ABOUT THE EXPERIMENT

Why study parallel heat transfer?

Hot industrial vessels lose heat through the steel wall and insulation before releasing it to the surroundings. Heat travels through the solid layers by conduction, while convection and thermal radiation remove heat simultaneously from the outer surface—making them parallel heat-transfer paths.

This experiment shows how emissivity, convection conditions, and insulation thickness affect heat loss and outer-surface temperature. The goal is to understand how insulation conserves energy while keeping the vessel surface within a safe temperature limit.

HEAT TRANSFER MODEL

Model and governing equations

Internal diameter1.00 m
Straight length6.00 m
Steel thickness0.025 m
Steel conductivity45 W/(m·K)
Ambient temperature, T∞25 °C
Safety limit, Ts60 °C
Rsteel = ln(rs/ri) ÷ (2πLksteel)
Rins = ln(ro/rs) ÷ (2πLkins)
Q̇conv = hAo(Ts − T∞)
Q̇rad = εσAo(Ts,K4 − T∞,K4)
hrad = εσ(Ts,K + Tsur,K)(Ts,K2 + Tsur,K2)
Q̇rad = hradAo(Ts − Tsur)
Q̇cond = Q̇conv + Q̇rad

Tin = 800 °C is imposed directly at the inner steel surface; fluid-side film resistance is neglected. The model solves the outer-surface temperature by balancing conduction through the steel and insulation with convection and radiation from the same outer-surface node. Celsius inputs are converted to kelvin inside the radiation calculation.

Radiation is modeled to large isothermal surroundings at Tsur = T∞ = 25 °C. The equivalent hrad above is exact for this model and is recalculated at each surface temperature. Both paths share the same area and temperature difference, so their coefficients can be compared directly. For a small temperature difference, hrad ≈ 4εσTm3, where Tm is the mean absolute surface and surroundings temperature.

Material conductivities are held constant. Real insulation conductivity can vary with temperature; 0.080 W/(m·K) is an instructional assumption, not a material specification across this temperature range.

ε = 0 and h = 0 are idealized limiting cases used to isolate the two outer-surface heat-transfer paths.
